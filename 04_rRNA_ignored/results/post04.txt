🔬 #TransparentScience | Can expression increase without gaining a single read?

In the previous post, I showed that a few highly abundant features can account for a substantial portion of a sequencing library while barely changing genome-wide correlations.

Now consider what happens when those features never enter the count matrix.

In replicate 11, the original paper reported that 31.21% of all reads came from rRNA. But the coding-gene count matrix does not contain that rRNA component. The coding-gene counts themselves remain unchanged.

What changes is the denominator against which their relative abundance is interpreted.
Imagine that one gene represents 1% of the complete library.
If the 31% rRNA component is omitted, the remaining 69% is treated as the new total. The same gene now represents approximately 1.45% of that smaller total.
It did not gain a single read.
Its calculated share simply increased by 45%.

📊 The figure shows this across all detected coding genes.

The blue curve includes a synthetic rRNA component calibrated to the paper’s reported 31% value. The orange curve uses only the coding-gene total.

The shape of the distribution remains the same. Every coding gene moves by the same amount: approximately 0.54 on the log2 scale.

★ Nothing in the orange curve is biologically upregulated. The denominator became smaller. ★

This is a basic property of compositional data: every measured share depends on what else is included in—or excluded from—the total.

Within one sample, the change is uniform. Across samples, however, the shift can differ because their rRNA fractions differ. A sample with 1% rRNA and a sample with 31% rRNA therefore do not experience the same rescaling when rRNA is absent from the count matrix.

This figure is a sensitivity analysis, not a reconstruction of the real rRNA count matrix. I used the paper’s published rRNA percentage.

Why does this matter?
Some normalization methods depend directly on the total number of counted reads. Others, such as DESeq2’s median-of-ratios approach, estimate what happened to the typical gene and should be much less sensitive to one dominant omitted feature.
