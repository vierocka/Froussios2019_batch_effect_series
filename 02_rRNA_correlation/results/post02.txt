🔬 #TransparentScience | How large can a hidden QC problem become? 

(My batch effect series)

Continuing with the 2019 Bioinformatics benchmarking dataset from last week: 17 Arabidopsis Col-0 biological replicates were generated across three experiments.

I reprocessed the raw sequencing data using the current TAIR10 Ensembl Plants annotation and an updated STAR–featureCounts–DESeq2 workflow.
I started with rRNA.

📊 Panel A: 
Across the 14 replicates from experiments A and B, the rRNA fraction reported in Supplementary Table S2C ranges from 1.3% to 31.2%.
That is an approximately 25-fold difference among samples representing the same genotype and biological condition.
The reported values agree with the pattern recovered in my reprocessing.

★ In one library, almost one-third of all reads originated from rRNA. ★

But can we identify the high-rRNA libraries based on their genome-wide expression correlations?

📊 Panel B: 
After DESeq2 rlog transformation, I calculated each sample’s mean Pearson correlation with the other 13 samples using:
● all 27,659 genes
● 27,655 genes after removing the four annotated rRNA genes
● random subsets of 5,000 genes, averaged across 50 draws
The three curves are almost indistinguishable.

Removing the annotated rRNA genes changes virtually nothing. Randomly reducing the matrix to 5,000 genes produces the same pattern. Even the libraries with the highest rRNA fractions remain within the same extremely narrow correlation range as the cleaner samples. 

The authors’ original correlation analysis did identify replicate 11 as an outlier. 

My reanalysis asks a different question:
☆ After normalization and transformation, can genome-wide correlation tell us which libraries contain high rRNA fractions? ☆

Here, it cannot.

Correlation measures whether the overall expression pattern is preserved. It does not measure how much of each library contains the RNA population we intended to quantify.

A correlation close to 1 can therefore coexist with a substantial difference in library composition.
