🔬 #TransparentScience | What is my RNA-seq series really about?

Over the past few weeks, I have been exploring how apparently small analytical decisions can change what we see in RNA-seq data.

The data come from Froussios et al. (2019, Bioinformatics): 17 biological replicates of the same Arabidopsis thaliana Col-0 genotype, generated across three experiments, all grown under the same nominal condition. Randomly constructed groups should therefore contain no designed biological difference.

So far:
▪️ rRNA content ranging from below 1% to over 30% remained almost invisible to genome-wide correlation.
▪️ Omitting a dominant feature changed every gene’s calculated share without changing a single raw count.
▪️ Total-count scaling changed DE calls when a dominant feature was added; DESeq2’s median-of-ratios did not.
▪️ One arbitrary split aligned with structure already present in the gene counts.

The labels had no designed biological meaning, yet DESeq2 detected hundreds of genes because the selected samples already differed in a way the design did not describe. The pipeline detects the contrast it was given. But it was not a contrast I designed. Where does the contrast come from?

This is what the series is really about:

★ QC is not simply a report generated before the “real” analysis. It is part of scientific decision-making. ★

The same applies to statistical thresholds.
padj < 0.05 is a useful decision rule—not a biological boundary.

Effect size, sample structure, experimental metadata, and the model's assumptions still matter.

Gold-standard workflows are valuable baselines, not universal ground truth. Their assumptions still have to match the dataset.

The point is to keep asking:
▪️ What information entered the analysis—and what was left out?
▪️ Do the samples contain structure that the design does not describe?
▪️ Do QC observations actually change our decisions?
▪️ Would the conclusion survive another reasonable analytical choice?

I am curious: when rRNA content, RNA degradation, or another QC metric looks unusual, does it change your pipeline—or does it mainly remain in the QC report?
