🔬 #TransparentScience | Does normalization change how many genes look differentially expressed?

Previously (https://lnkd.in/p/eBTs3Wg7), I showed a compositional effect: when a dominant feature such as rRNA is absent from the gene-count matrix used to calculate library shares, every counted gene occupies a larger share (without gaining a single read). 

Does that change DE calls?

I used the seven ExpA replicates from Froussios et al. (2019, Bioinformatics): 
Same genotype, same experimental batch, and no designed group difference.
I sampled eight 3-vs-3 splits, leaving one sample out each time, and ran DESeq2. Replicate 11 (with 31.2% reported rRNA) was excluded from the original analysis. I therefore used replicate 6 (the highest reported rRNA fraction among the retained ExpA samples, 23.7%). I placed it in group 2 in every split. 

For each split, I estimated size factors four ways:
▪️ DESeq2 median-of-ratios or total-count scaling
▪️ With or without a synthetic rRNA feature based on the paper’s published percentages

The same gene counts were tested every time. Only the size factors changed.

📊 Using padj < 0.05:
DESeq2 median-of-ratios produced exactly the same number of DE genes with and without the synthetic rRNA feature.
Total-count scaling changed the result in four of eight splits, in both directions.
Synthetic rRNA included → ignored:
23 → 8
0 → 2
1 → 0
503 → 615 genes

★ Same gene counts. Same model. Different normalization. Different numbers of DE calls. ★

Why?
▪️ Total-count scaling uses the sum of the features supplied for each sample. Adding a dominant feature therefore changes its size factor directly.
▪️ DESeq2 estimates the size factor from the typical gene-wise ratio. One additional feature among thousands has almost no influence on the median.

This is a sensitivity analysis, not a reconstruction of the original rRNA molecules. It examines eight sampled splits out of 60 possible comparisons and uses one synthetic rRNA feature calibrated to Supplementary Table S2C. 

One line in the figure is difficult to ignore.
Split 6 produced more than 500 DE genes under every normalization strategy.

That is a different phenomenon.
Split 6 separates replicates 1–3 from replicates 4–6. Group 2 contains the three highest-rRNA ExpA replicates, while group 1 contains most of the lowest ones. This may also align with processing order.

The synthetic rRNA feature did not create that structure.  It was already present in the real gene counts.
