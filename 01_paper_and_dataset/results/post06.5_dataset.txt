The Froussios et al. study was specifically designed as a same-condition RNA-seq benchmark, not as a treatment-versus-control experiment.
Arabidopsis thaliana is the reference species of plant biology. Its genome is small: only 5 chromosomes, about 120 Mb in total (TAIR10). Yet its transcriptome and proteome are complex: about 27,600 protein-coding genes give rise to more than 48,000 annotated transcripts.
With no designed biological difference, this dataset exposes the variation that interferes and covaries with real biology: batch effects between the three experiments, differences in rRNA content, possible RNA degradation, and natural biological variability between plants, which shows up as overdispersion in the counts.
Experimental design
- Organism: Arabidopsis thaliana Col-0, all wild type.
- 17 biological replicates produced in three separate experimental runs:
  - ExpA: replicates 1–7
  - ExpB: replicates 8–14
  - ExpC: replicates 15–17
- Seeds were grown aseptically on MS10 plates.
- Stratified for two days at 4°C.
- Grown for another 14 days at 21°C under a 16-hour light/8-hour dark cycle.
- Seedlings were then harvested for total RNA extraction.
- ERCC spike-ins were added.
- Libraries were prepared with Illumina TruSeq Stranded Total RNA with Ribo-Zero Plant.
- Sequencing used 100-bp paired-end reads on a HiSeq 2000.
The researchers used these same-condition replicates to:
1. Test which statistical distribution best describes gene counts.
2. Assess false-positive control in nine differential-expression tools.
3. Construct artificial comparisons between groups of biological replicates for which no biological difference was designed.
Replicate 11 was later excluded because it correlated poorly with the other samples.
